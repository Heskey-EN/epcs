Every private landlord in England will soon have to put their rental properties on a national register, and your Energy Performance Certificate is one of the documents you'll be asked for. The government confirmed the timetable on 9 and 10 September 2026. If your rental is anywhere in the North West – Lancashire, Greater Manchester, Merseyside, Cheshire or Cumbria – you have until **14 August 2027** to register it.

That sounds a long way off. But the register is the first time your EPC, your gas safety record and your electrical certificate will sit side by side in one place that councils can check. It's worth getting your paperwork in order well before the deadline.

## What is the "Register your rental property" service?

It's the new private rented sector database created by the Renters' Rights Act, now being introduced as the second phase of that law. Landlords of properties let on assured or regulated tenancies will have to record who they are, what they let, who lives there, the rent they charge and the safety and energy documents that go with each home.

The government says registering will be a legal requirement and that landlords who don't register risk a fine. Once the service is running, landlords and letting agents will also need to show a registration number on their property adverts.

At the moment only properties that are actually let need registering. The government has said future legislation will require empty properties to be registered before they are marketed too.

## Key dates for North West landlords

The service opens nationally on **15 December 2026**, and you can register from that date whichever region you're in. The legal requirement then switches on one region at a time, with a three-month window to register:

| Region | Rules commence | Deadline to register |
|---|---|---|
| West Midlands | 15 December 2026 | 14 March 2027 |
| East of England | 15 January 2027 | 14 April 2027 |
| East Midlands | 15 February 2027 | 14 May 2027 |
| South East | 15 March 2027 | 14 June 2027 |
| Yorkshire and the Humber | 15 April 2027 | 14 July 2027 |
| **North West** | **15 May 2027** | **14 August 2027** |
| North East | 15 June 2027 | 14 September 2027 |
| London | 15 July 2027 | 14 October 2027 |
| South West | 15 August 2027 | 14 November 2027 |

The deadline depends on where the property is, not where you live. A Blackpool landlord who lives in London still works to the North West date for a Blackpool flat.

## What you'll need to provide

According to the government's guidance, you'll be asked for:

- **Your details** – name, address and contact information
- **Property details** – address, property type, number of bedrooms and occupancy
- **Tenancy information** – the number of occupants and households, and whether it's furnished
- **Rent** – the amount and how often it's paid
- **Safety and energy documents** – your gas safety record, electrical certificate (EICR or EIC) and a copy of your most recent EPC, along with details of any minimum energy efficiency (MEES) exemption you rely on

There's an annual fee of **£65 per property**, which has to be renewed every year. The fee is adjusted pro rata while the service rolls out.

## Why your EPC matters more once the register is live

Right now, a rental home in England or Wales must have an EPC rating of **E or above** to be let, unless it has a registered exemption. Councils can fine landlords up to £5,000 per property for breaching those rules.

That minimum is going up. In January 2026 the government confirmed that privately rented homes will need to reach **EPC C by 1 October 2030**, with a spending cap of £10,000 per property. Elmhurst Energy's summary of the response notes that improvement work carried out from October 2025 onwards counts towards that cap.

Put those two things together and the register becomes more than admin. It puts a property's EPC data next to its tenancy details in one national system. The National Residential Landlords Association has said it wants the database to work as a real compliance tool for tenants and councils, not just a directory. If that's how it ends up being used, an out-of-date certificate or a rating that doesn't reflect your property will be easier for other people to spot.

## What we'd do now

As Domestic Energy Assessors working across Preston, Blackpool and the wider North West, this is the order we'd suggest:

1. **Look up every certificate you hold.** Search your postcodes on the government's [Find an energy certificate](https://find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk/) service and note each rating and expiry date. EPCs last 10 years, so a certificate issued before August 2017 will have expired by your registration deadline.
2. **Compare the EPC with the property as it is today.** An older certificate won't include a new boiler, extra loft insulation, double glazing or heating controls fitted since. If you've made improvements, a new assessment records them.
3. **Flag anything rated D or below.** You don't need to hit C for the register, but the 2030 deadline is close enough that it's worth knowing what's needed and roughly what it will cost. An assessment will show which improvements make the biggest difference.
4. **Collect your gas safety record and EICR** for each property, so you can upload everything in one go.
5. **Budget for £65 per property per year**, and sign up for GOV.UK email alerts so you see the step-by-step registration guidance when it's published.

Registration opens on 15 December 2026, so there's time to deal with an expired or inaccurate EPC calmly rather than in the last few weeks before August 2027.
